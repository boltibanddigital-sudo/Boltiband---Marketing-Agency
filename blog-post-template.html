How We Took a Local Service Blog From Zero to #3 on Google in 30 Days

Palak Bhatia
July 9, 2026 · 8 min read

Most local service businesses treat their blog as an afterthought — a few posts written for the sake of having a blog, with no real strategy behind them. When a Delhi-NCR laundry and dry cleaning brand came to us, that was exactly the pattern we saw: no organic visibility, and blog content that wasn't mapped to what customers were actually searching for.

Within 30 days of publishing a single, properly structured post, that page was ranking #3 on Google for a high-intent local keyword — and it's been generating leads at zero ad spend ever since. Here's exactly how we did it.

Step 1: Finding the Right Keyword

Before writing a single word, we mapped search intent. Generic terms like "dry cleaning" are dominated by national aggregators and impossible to rank for quickly. Instead, we looked for keywords that were specific enough to have lower competition, but still had genuine commercial intent behind them — the kind of phrase someone types right before they're ready to book.

We used Search Console and Ahrefs to cross-reference search volume against ranking difficulty, and settled on a long-tail, locally-modified keyword that competitors were largely ignoring.

What we looked for

  • Search volume high enough to matter, but not so high that it attracts national competition
  • Clear commercial or local intent — not just informational curiosity
  • A gap in the top 10 results where existing content was thin, outdated, or poorly structured

Step 2: Structuring Content Around Intent, Not Word Count

A lot of SEO advice pushes writers toward hitting an arbitrary word count. We ignored that. Instead, we structured the post to directly answer the question behind the search — with the most useful information appearing first, not buried under generic filler.

"Rank on page one for the terms your customers are searching, and stay there." — this only works when the content actually answers the query better than what's already ranking.

That meant clear H2s mapped to sub-questions a real customer would have, short paragraphs, and a comparison structure that made scanning easy on mobile — where most local searches happen.

Step 3: On-Page Fixes That Mattered

Content alone doesn't rank. We paired the post with a set of technical and on-page fixes:

  • Schema markup for the service and local business, so Google could understand context beyond the text
  • Internal linking from existing high-authority pages on the site into the new post
  • Core Web Vitals fixes — the page was loading slowly on mobile, which we resolved by compressing images and deferring non-critical scripts
  • Title tag and meta description rewrites designed to improve click-through rate, not just target the keyword

Step 4: Tracking and Iterating

We didn't publish and walk away. Weekly rank tracking showed the post moving from unranked to page two within 10 days, then into the top five by day 21. At that point, we made small on-page adjustments based on which related queries Search Console showed we were partially ranking for — capturing additional long-tail traffic without a full rewrite.

The Takeaway

This wasn't a lucky ranking. It was the result of picking the right keyword, structuring content around genuine search intent, fixing the technical issues that were holding the page back, and tracking performance closely enough to iterate fast. That's the same system we run for every SEO client — just applied to whatever the data tells us matters most for their business.
